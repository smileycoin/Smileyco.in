08. Characteristics of the crypto-asset

This white paper has been prepared by Styrktarfélagið Broskallar, using the registered foreign name SmileyCharity, to provide information about SmileyCoin and its use in the SmileyProject. SmileyCharity is responsible for the content of this document. It does not seek admission to trading. The following particulars identify SmileyCharity as its preparer and as an organisation materially involved in the educational activities described.

SmileyCoin (SMLY) is the native crypto-asset of the SmileyCoin blockchain, introduced in 2014 through research and development at the University of Iceland to reward educational achievement in SmileyTutor, formerly tutor-web. Its founding purpose was to investigate and support educational incentives, rather than promise financial returns. SmileyTutor is an open-source learning environment grounded in a programme of peer-reviewed research. Studies support the educational value of the learning system and separately report that SMLY reward levels influenced exercise participation; they do not establish that cryptocurrency rewards alone caused improved learning outcomes.[D-1–3][D-2][D-3]

SmileyCharity, registered in Iceland as Styrktarfélagið Broskallar, uses SmileyTutor and SMLY to support educational access in disadvantaged communities. Through participating schools and community libraries, students receive access to learning devices and local assistance, earn SMLY through educational activities, and may spend their rewards through participating SmileyStores. Under the established virtual-store model, local purchases are facilitated by programme representatives and reimbursed by SmileyCharity in local currency. Research describing operations in Kenyan communities and refugee camps reported that the programme had reached 5,000 students by June 2024.[D-4]

The blockchain, the educational allocation administered by the SmileyCoinFund, and SmileyCharity’s activities are distinct. The blockchain records transfers and applies protocol rules, while the Fund supports educational rewards and SmileyCharity administers its own programmes and funding. SMLY holdings and protocol-directed allocations do not replace the fiat funding needed to purchase goods and operate the charity. An independent evaluation reported in 2024 rated the charitable programme highly for relevance, coherence, efficiency and effectiveness, while identifying sustainability, governance, financing and outcome measurement as areas requiring further attention. Long-term impact was not rated.[D-5] Initiatives including SmileyTrees and SmileyKuku are testing approaches to greater financial sustainability; self-sufficiency has not been established.

Educational purpose and documented use do not eliminate crypto-asset risks. Access to programme goods and services depends on local arrangements, eligibility, funding and continued operation. Programme reference valuations are neither guaranteed market prices nor a general right to redeem SMLY for fiat currency. SMLY may lose some or all of its value, become illiquid or cease to be transferable. Neither the research nor the independent evaluation constitutes regulatory approval, a blockchain security audit or a promise of investment returns.

Through the EduChain R&D project, SmileyCoin were also made available on the BNB chain through a smart contract. These 1 bn BNB.SMLY correspond exactly to 1 bn SMLY on the original SMLY blockchain, held in cold storage. This whitepaper represents both SMLY and BNB.SMLY.

The 1bn BNB.SMLY are set up in token contract 0xe015f8C012e932F13184061F3ffd956E9000A6CD and a 1-1 swap mechanism against SMLY is made available on Hybrix.io. There is no guarantee that this swap mechanism will operate indefinitely.

A.13 Business activity

Purpose and principal activities

SmileyCharity is an Icelandic non-profit association whose registered purpose is to support and empower students in impoverished areas through access to education, food and other necessities. Its principal activities include obtaining grants and donations, providing learning equipment, and supporting educational participation through partner schools and community libraries.

Through the SmileyProject, including activities known as Education in a Suitcase, participating students obtain access to SmileyTutor, formerly tutor-web, and local educational support. Students can earn SmileyCoin (SMLY) through educational activities and use their rewards under participating SmileyStore arrangements. These activities aim to reduce barriers to education and support progression towards further study, including university.

Research and operational basis

The educational platform and its use of cryptocurrency rewards have been described in peer-reviewed research. Stefansson and Lentin (2017) [A-1] document the introduction and early educational use of SMLY; Stefansson and colleagues (2024)[A-2] describe blockchain services supporting educational activities and participating stores in Kenyan communities and refugee camps. These publications document the development and use of the technologies; they do not establish guaranteed educational outcomes, financial returns or future programme availability.

Under the established virtual-store model, local programme representatives facilitate eligible purchases and SmileyCharity provides local-currency reimbursement under the programme’s arrangements. Access depends on eligibility, local implementation, available funding and continued operation. This model is distinct from physical stores purchasing and reselling products directly for SMLY.

Relationship to other project participants

SmileyCharity’s charitable activities are distinct from the operation of the SmileyCoin blockchain and the administration of the educational allocation by the SmileyCoinFund. SmileyTutor names the educational platform; SmileyTutor NGO is a separate non-profit association registered in Iceland (ID no. 570426-1330). SmileyInvest is a separate private limited company established to purchase SMLY on cryptocurrency markets in support of the broader project. Their activities and resources should not be treated as activities or assets of SmileyCharity merely because they support related objectives.

The technologies originated in research and development at the University of Iceland. That research relationship does not itself establish University responsibility for SmileyCharity’s obligations or a guarantee of SMLY’s value.

Part B – Information about the issuer, if different from the offeror or person seeking admission to trading

Applicability and distinction between project participants

This white paper does not identify a person or undertaking as the issuer of native SmileyCoin (SMLY). SmileyCharity is identified in Part A as the preparer of this document and as an organisation conducting educational and charitable activities involving SMLY; that identification does not, by itself, establish issuer status.

The project history described in D.4 and G.5 distinguishes the original creation of SMLY and its initial educational allocation from subsequent generation of coins under the blockchain’s consensus rules and transfers of already-created coins through grants, educational rewards and other transactions. Software development, administration of an educational allocation, receipt of protocol-directed allocations and distribution of existing SMLY are distinct activities and should not be treated as interchangeable.

The absence of an identifiable issuer reflects the origin of both the educational platform and the crypto-asset in open academic research rather than in a corporate project. The learning environment in which SMLY is earned was designed from the outset as a freely accessible, open-source and open-content system with no commercial components, developed by a consortium of academic contributors across several countries [B-1]. Its architecture, licensing and absence of institutional ownership were documented before the crypto-asset existed [B-2]. SmileyCoin was introduced in 2014 as a reward mechanism within that platform, as described by Stefansson and Lentin (2017), cited in D.4. These publications provide historical and technical context for why no single person or undertaking stands in the position of issuer; they do not by themselves determine issuer status under Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, and the project’s origins before that Regulation do not establish the absence of an identifiable issuer.

The Financial Supervisory Authority’s letter of 11 May 2023, reference 2304061, assessed the activities described to it under Icelandic Act No. 140/2018. Its conclusion concerning SmileyCharity and Smiley Invest ehf. was a historical assessment under that legislation, not a determination of issuer status under MiCA. See A.13.

SmileyCharity has assessed the applicability of Part B and concludes that no person or undertaking can be identified as the issuer of native SMLY. The genesis block was mined on 2014-11-13 as part of academic research at the University of Iceland. Since then, new SMLY has been generated only under the blockchain’s consensus rules, through blocks produced by independent miners, and no legal person controls issuance. B.1 is therefore reported as TRUE and fields B.2 to B.12 as not applicable.

BNB.SMLY was developed as an extension of SMLY, corresponding to moving existing SMLY, owned by the SmileyCharity, to run on an alternate chain (the BNB Smart Chain).

The contract was deployed by Hybrix, which holds the owner role (see B.1 and H.6). The full supply was minted directly to SmileyCharity. The tokens on the new chain therefore have an exact matching number of tokens held in cold storage on the native chain. Because the native reserve matches the BNB.SMLY supply, it is technically possible for the reserve holder to convert all outstanding BNB.SMLY back into native SMLY. However, no formal redemption mechanism exists, holders have no enforceable right to conversion, and conversion currently depends on third-party services and on the reserve holder (see G.1, G.14 and I.3). SmileyCharity treats BNB.SMLY as a representation of existing native SMLY rather than a new issuance. This is SmileyCharity’s own assessment, not a regulatory determination. The reasons for this implementation lie in research and development for education: A research project (EduChain) was set up in part to investigate the feasibility of using a blockchain to host drills, allocate drill items and store grades, all using on-chain operations. As parts of this project, experiments were conducted on multiple chains, but BNB.SMLY was the final output of the project. The BNB.SMLY technical implementation is described in H.6.

D.4 Crypto-asset project description

Background: educational purpose, research and operational context

SmileyCoin (SMLY) is the original native crypto-asset of the SmileyCoin blockchain. It was introduced in 2014 through research and development at the University of Iceland as a mechanism for rewarding educational achievement in SmileyTutor, formerly known as tutor-web. Its founding purpose was to investigate and support educational incentives, rather than to promise financial returns to purchasers. The development, initial classroom deployment, student adoption and early experiences with cryptocurrency rewards are described by Stefansson and Lentin (2017).[D-1]

SmileyTutor is an open-source learning environment, with particular emphasis on mathematics and statistics. It combines exercises, feedback and adaptive question allocation to support learning rather than merely assess existing knowledge. Its development rests on a research programme documented in peer-reviewed publications beginning with Stefansson (2004).[D-2] A repeated randomised crossover study conducted in an introductory statistics course between 2011 and 2014 found that students in the 2014 experiment achieved higher test scores after completing tutor-web homework than after completing pen-and-paper homework (Jonsdottir, Bjornsdottir and Stefansson, 2017).[D-3] This evidence concerns the educational system and should not be interpreted as showing that cryptocurrency rewards alone caused those learning gains. Research on SmileyCoin separately reports evidence that reward magnitude influenced the number of exercise attempts in the studied setting.[D-1]

The claim that the learning environment produces learning, rather than merely measuring it, rests on a sequence of peer-reviewed analyses that predate the crypto-asset. Analysis of responses to repeated drill items, including items containing randomly generated numbers that cannot be answered from memory, showed a statistically significant increase in the probability of a correct answer between a student’s first and second encounter with an item; for the random-number items the proportion correct rose from 67 % to 82 % [D-9]. The same data were used to show that classical item-response models designed for testing do not fit a dynamic drilling environment because they omit a learning term, and a model including such a term estimated the first-to-second-attempt improvement at roughly 62 % to 86 % for a typical item [D-10, D-11]. These results concern the educational system in Icelandic university courses between 2003 and 2009. They indicate that improvement within the drilling system, the same system whose grades now trigger SMLY rewards, reflects learning and not only memorised answers. They do not establish that any particular level of reward is necessary or sufficient for that learning, and they are not evidence of any financial characteristic of SMLY.

SmileyCharity, legally registered in Iceland as Styrktarfélagið Broskallar, was established in 2015 to support educational access for students in disadvantaged communities. Through its SmileyProject, participating schools and community libraries—called SmileyLibraries—provide access to learning devices, SmileyTutor and local support. SmileyGuides, normally teachers or librarians, assist participating students. Students earn SMLY through educational activities and can withdraw earned rewards to their own wallets.

Participating SmileyStores provide opportunities to spend these rewards on items such as learning devices, food, school supplies, sanitary products and connectivity. Under the established virtual-store model, students transfer SMLY to a designated store address and a SmileyGuide facilitates the corresponding purchase. SmileyCharity provides local-currency reimbursement under the programme’s arrangements. On-chain store registration and transaction records support payment verification and reconciliation; they do not, by themselves, prove that physical goods have been delivered. The technical implementation and its use in Kenyan communities and refugee camps are described by Stefansson and colleagues (2024), who reported that the programme had reached 5,000 students by June 2024.[D-4] By 11 October 2026, 9,496 users in African and Lebanese SmileyLibraries had used the system since first recorded use, and 3,670 had been active in the preceding twelve months (Tables D-1 and D-2).

SmileyCoin’s blockchain and the charitable programme are distinct. The blockchain records transfers and applies its consensus and issuance rules. SmileyCharity administers its own educational activities, funding and participating-site arrangements; these activities do not constitute control over all uses of SMLY. The blockchain was initially derived from Litecoin source code and subsequently incorporated multiple proof-of-work algorithms and features supporting educational applications.[D-1][D-4]

The SmileyCoinFund administers the remaining educational allocation originating from the initial premine through a multisignature arrangement and a governing mandate. It has provided SMLY grants to support SmileyTutor rewards. Separately, SmileyCharity receives protocol-directed allocations of newly generated SMLY through designated addresses, referred to within the project as income streams. The protocol also allocates newly generated coins to miners and qualifying holder addresses. These mechanisms and their historical development are described in the SmileyCoin research publications.[D-1][D-4] Token holdings and protocol-generated allocations are distinct from the fiat funding needed to purchase goods and operate the charitable programme.

An independent evaluation conducted by Verkís and a Kenyan evaluation specialist, commissioned by Iceland’s Ministry for Foreign Affairs and reported in 2024, assessed SmileyCharity’s Education in a Suitcase activities over 2017–2023. It rated relevance, coherence, efficiency and effectiveness Highly Satisfactory, while rating sustainability Adequate to Satisfactory. It recommended stronger institutional governance, diversified financing and improved outcome measurement. Long-term impact was not rated because it was considered too early to assess.[5] This evaluation concerns the charitable programme, not regulatory approval of SMLY or an audit of blockchain security.

Work towards greater financial sustainability includes SmileyTrees and SmileyKuku, which combine supervised agricultural education with trials of local production and trade using SMLY. Proposed expansion of these activities and physical SmileyStores is intended to test whether they can reduce dependence on charitable reimbursement; financial self-sufficiency has not been established. Separate supporting initiatives include SmileyInvest, a private limited company established to purchase SMLY on exchanges, and SmileyTutor NGO, whose planned advertising-funded app model would use advertising income to purchase SMLY for educational rewards. These arrangements are described separately in the project, funding and conflict-of-interest disclosures.

Part H – information on the underlying technology

H.1 Distributed ledger technology (DTL)

Native SmileyCoin blockchain

Native SmileyCoin (SMLY) operates on a public, permissionless blockchain initially derived from Litecoin source code. Transactions are propagated between participating nodes and recorded in cryptographically linked blocks. Full nodes independently check transactions and blocks against the consensus rules implemented by their software.

Native SMLY uses a transaction-output model in which transactions spend eligible previous outputs and create new outputs subject to specified spending conditions. Wallet balances are derived from the outputs that the wallet can identify and spend.

The project describes a target average interval of three minutes between blocks and five supported proof-of-work algorithms: SHA256d, Scrypt, Groestl, Qubit and Skein. The target interval is not a guaranteed transaction-confirmation time. Consensus operation is described further in H.4.

Transaction records are publicly inspectable through compatible nodes and blockchain explorers. Addresses are pseudonymous: transaction information may be linked to individuals through other information. Public accessibility does not establish that every explorer is accurate or continuously available.

SmileyCoin’s development and early educational reward applications are described by Stefansson and Lentin (2017).[H-1] Subsequent applications include service registration and payment records supporting educational programmes, described by Stefansson and colleagues (2024).[H-2]

BNB.SMLY

BNB.SMLY is a separate token representation at contract address 0xe015f8C012e932F13184061F3ffd956E9000A6CD on the BNB-chain network identified in D.4. It is not a native output on the SmileyCoin blockchain.

BNB.SMLY transfers depend on the host network and token contract. Conversion into native SMLY additionally depends on the reserve and conversion arrangements. The existence of records on two blockchains does not, by itself, establish an automatic or trustless connection between them.

H.2 Protocols and technical standards

Native-network protocols

SmileyCoin uses a peer-to-peer network protocol derived from its Litecoin code ancestry to exchange transactions, blocks and related network information. Consensus rules determine which transactions and blocks are valid. Node relay policies, miner transaction-selection policies and wallet defaults are separate from consensus validity.

Native transactions specify inputs, outputs and spending conditions. Digital signatures authorise relevant spending operations; cryptographic hashes support transaction identification and the linking and verification of blocks. The supported mining algorithms are listed in H.1.

Multisignature spending is used for the educational allocation described in G.5. The applicable signature threshold and responsible signatories belong to that allocation’s control arrangements; they are not a governance mechanism for the entire blockchain.

Application data

The project describes the use of transaction data outputs, including OP_RETURN, for application messages. Compatible application software interprets those messages for functions such as service discovery and coupon-related communication.

OP_RETURN does not itself encrypt information or execute a general-purpose application. Where a message is encrypted, encryption and decryption occur through the relevant application’s cryptographic implementation. Network validation of a transaction does not establish that an application message is accurate or that a related off-chain service has been performed.

BNB.SMLY

BNB.SMLY’s technical interface and supported operations are determined by its deployed contract and host network: BEP-20 (ERC-20 compatible), 18 decimals, fixed supply, no mint/burn/pause/blacklist functions.

A shared ticker or compatible wallet interface does not make native SMLY and BNB.SMLY interchangeable at the transaction-protocol level.

H.3 Technology used

Native-network infrastructure

Native SMLY depends on node software that stores and validates ledger information, mining software and hardware that produce candidate blocks, and wallets that construct and authorise transactions.

Mining and full-node validation are distinct functions. A participant can validate ledger information without mining. The computational resources required for competitive mining are also distinct from those needed to operate a wallet or non-mining node.

Wallets and key management

Self-custody wallets require secure key generation, storage and backup using the recovery method supported by the particular wallet. Not every wallet necessarily uses a mnemonic recovery phrase. A custodial account instead depends on the provider’s key management and account records.

The Fund’s multisignature arrangement and the reported cold storage of native SMLY backing BNB.SMLY are separate custody arrangements. “Cold storage” describes aspects of key handling; it does not, by itself, establish effective recovery procedures, independent control or legal segregation.

Educational applications

SmileyTutor reward administration, participating-store interfaces and coupon services combine blockchain transactions with application software and off-chain operations. Blockchain records can support payment verification and reconciliation, but learning assessment, eligibility decisions, goods delivery and charitable reimbursement are not performed by blockchain consensus.

The on-chain service applications described by Stefansson and colleagues (2024) illustrate this division between ledger functionality and educational service delivery.[H-2]

BNB.SMLY and external infrastructure

BNB.SMLY additionally depends on its host-network (**BNB Smart Chain*) clients, token contract and compatible wallets. Conversion services introduce further software, operational and custody dependencies. Failure of an interface or conversion service may prevent access to that service even while one or both underlying blockchains remain operational.

H.4 Consensus mechanism

Native SMLY: multi-algorithm proof of work

Native SmileyCoin uses proof of work with the supported algorithms listed in H.1. Miners construct candidate blocks and search for a proof satisfying the applicable algorithm-specific difficulty target.

Receiving full nodes verify the proof and the remaining consensus conditions, including transaction validity and permitted issuance and allocation. A valid proof of work alone does not make an otherwise invalid block acceptable.

Difficulty adjustment is intended to regulate block production around the stated three-minute average target. Actual intervals vary with mining participation and network conditions.

Competing valid branches are evaluated using the chain-selection rules implemented by SmileyCoin nodes. S.4 describes selection by accumulated proof of work.

Native transaction finality is probabilistic. Additional confirmations generally increase confidence that a transaction will remain in the accepted history, but reorganisations remain possible. The appropriate confirmation policy depends on transaction value, network conditions and the recipient’s risk assessment.

Supporting multiple mining algorithms does not guarantee independent miners, evenly distributed mining power or immunity to attacks. Security depends on the implemented rules, participating hash power, its distribution and the integrity of software and network communications.

BNB.SMLY

BNB.SMLY does not use native SmileyCoin mining to validate its token transfers. Their ordering, execution and finality depend on the host blockchain’s consensus rules. Host-chain confirmation does not verify the native reserve balance or ensure completion of conversion into native SMLY.

Reward-Gaming and Participant Behaviour

Because SMLY is distributed in response to performance in an open, freely accessible learning environment, the reward mechanism is exposed to behaviour that the educational system was not primarily designed to resist. Peer-reviewed studies of the platform show that students work towards a high internal grade and that their decision to stop or continue is strongly driven by the grading scheme; in a large calculus course, the probability of stopping rose sharply once a top grade was reached, and the choice of grading window materially altered behaviour [I-1].

A separate analysis of the same platform found that students who answer quickly and incorrectly, consistent with guessing, show the least improvement between a status exam and a final exam, and that improvement is a non-linear function of both the number of items attempted and the time spent per item [I-2].

Once a crypto-asset reward was attached to the grade, the platform experienced repeated attempts by non-students using many registrations to extract SMLY by answering easy items; these were addressed by separating reward parameters for class-registered students from those for self-registered users and by other measures described in the literature [I-3].

The risk remains that reward parameters set to encourage genuine study may be exploited, that countermeasures may reduce rewards for legitimate students, and that the quantity of SMLY entering circulation through rewards may differ from the amounts intended by the SmileyCoinFund’s allocation. None of these studies measures the effect of reward value in fiat terms, and no inference should be drawn about the market behaviour of holders from the study behaviour of students.

Period consumption: 5 × 105.9 W × 4,344 h = 2,300 kWh. Annualised: 2,300 kWh × 2 = 4,600 kWh/year. Block count. During the period the ten project-operated containers produced 5,793 blocks (verified against the SmileyCoin ledger via smileycoin-cli on 10 October 2026). Network-wide upper bound. SmileyCoin adjusts difficulty separately for each of its five algorithms, so a miner’s share of an algorithm’s blocks equals its share of that algorithm’s hashrate. Project hosts (Intel Core i5-7500T CPUs) are among the least energy-efficient hardware per hash; any GPU or ASIC miner uses less energy per hash. Network energy for each algorithm is therefore bounded by project energy on that algorithm divided by the project’s share of that algorithm’s blocks. Project energy was split equally between the containers on each host, and each algorithm was assumed to produce one fifth of the approximately 86,880 blocks in the period. The bound is 128,700 kWh for the period (about 257,000 kWh/year), of which about 107,600 kWh comes from sha256d, where the project produced only 99 blocks. Actual consumption is expected to be far lower. Figures are not derived from other networks’ published estimates.
